PROJECT NAME: PortRisk — Global Port Delay Intelligence System
(Name is a suggestion, Aryan to confirm)

CORE PROBLEM STATEMENT:
Predict the risk and magnitude of container port delays worldwide using
publicly available data and principled synthetic generation — mirroring the
problem Maersk's PortSight platform solves commercially.

THREE PREDICTION TARGETS (layered system):
  Target A — Will this vessel call be delayed? (binary classification)
  Target B — How many days of delay? (regression)
  Target C — What is this port's risk score for the next 30 days? (0-100 score,
             derived from A + B, updated weekly)

--- REAL ANCHOR 1: World Bank Container Port Performance Index (CPPI) ---
URL: https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/fa57ba78-0402-4eb4-b168-51708cf526f7
What it is: Annual efficiency scores for 403 global container ports, 2020-2024
Key metric: Total port hours per vessel call (lower = better)
Coverage: 175,000+ vessel calls, 247 million container moves
License: CC BY 3.0 IGO (fully open, free to use with attribution)
Format: PDF report + linked underlying Excel/CSV dataset
How to get it: Download from World Bank Open Knowledge portal (no auth required)
What we use it for:
  - Base delay distribution per port (high CPPI score = tight distribution,
    low CPPI score = fat-tailed distribution)
  - Port efficiency ranking as a feature in ML models
  - Validation anchor for synthetic generator output

--- REAL ANCHOR 2: Geopolitical Risk (GPR) Index ---
URL: https://www.matteoiacoviello.com/gpr.htm
Also (newer, daily resolution): https://www.matteoiacoviello.com/ai_gpr.html
What it is: Monthly index of geopolitical risk built from newspaper article counts
Authors: Dario Caldara (Fed) + Matteo Iacoviello (Fed/Harvard)
Coverage: Monthly from 1985 to present. AI-GPR version: daily, 1960-present
Country-level: Yes, bilateral country pairs available
License: Freely available with attribution (academic, Fed working paper)
Format: Excel + CSV direct download, no auth required
How to get it: Direct download link on matteoiacoviello.com
What we use it for:
  - Shock injection into synthetic delay generator
  - GPR spike = delay distribution widens and shifts right at affected ports
  - Red Sea crisis (late 2023), COVID (2021), Suez (2021) all captured
  - Feature in ML models: GPR at port's country, month of voyage

SECTION 4 — SYNTHETIC DATA GENERATOR DESIGN
================================================================================

PURPOSE: Generate ~50,000 synthetic vessel call records (2020-2024) for
60 major global ports, with realistic delay distributions calibrated to
real-world anchors.

THIS IS NOT RANDOM DATA. Every distribution is justified and documented.

--- PORT SELECTION (60 ports) ---
Select top ports by TEU throughput across all major trade regions:
  Asia-Pacific (20): Shanghai, Singapore, Ningbo, Shenzhen, Busan, Hong Kong,
    Guangzhou, Qingdao, Tianjin, Port Klang, Tanjung Pelepas, Jakarta,
    Kaohsiung, Tokyo, Osaka, Manila, Ho Chi Minh City, Laem Chabang,
    Colombo, Mumbai
  Europe (15): Rotterdam, Antwerp, Hamburg, Felixstowe, Valencia, Algeciras,
    Piraeus, Genoa, Le Havre, Bremerhaven, Gdansk, Gioia Tauro,
    Barcelona, Marseille, Constanta
  Middle East / Africa (10): Jebel Ali, Port Said, Salalah, Dammam,
    Mombasa, Durban, Tanger Med, Aden, Bandar Abbas, Karachi
  Americas (15): Los Angeles, Long Beach, New York, Savannah, Houston,
    Santos, Manzanillo (MX), Cartagena (CO), Colon, Vancouver,
    Seattle, Baltimore, Charleston, Norfolk, Lazaro Cardenas

--- BASE DELAY DISTRIBUTION (calibrated to CPPI) ---
For each port, derive base parameters from CPPI score:

  CPPI score mapping (approximate, based on published CPPI data):
  - Top 10% ports (score > 80):   mean_delay = 0.5d,  std = 0.8d  (e.g. Yangshan, Tanjung Pelepas)
  - Top 25% ports (score 65-80):  mean_delay = 1.2d,  std = 1.5d
  - Middle 50% ports (score 40-65): mean_delay = 2.5d, std = 2.8d
  - Bottom 25% ports (score < 40): mean_delay = 4.5d,  std = 4.2d  (e.g. some LA, African ports)

  Use log-normal distribution (not normal) — delay can't be negative,
  real delay distributions are right-skewed. This is documented in maritime
  ops literature (cite: CPPI methodology note + Notteboom et al. 2020).

  Delay = 0 for ~35% of vessel calls (no delay) — calibrated from CPPI data
  showing average schedule reliability was ~65% in 2021-2022.

--- SHOCK INJECTION (calibrated to GPR + known events) ---
Apply multiplicative shock factor to delay distributions based on:

  1. GPR monthly index:
     - GPR < 100 (baseline): multiplier = 1.0
     - GPR 100-150: multiplier = 1.3
     - GPR 150-200: multiplier = 1.8
     - GPR > 200 (major event): multiplier = 2.5
     Geographic targeting: high GPR in region X → ports in that region affected

  2. Hard-coded known shock events (with date ranges and affected ports):
     - COVID-19 global disruption: Jan 2020 - Dec 2021
       Effect: All ports, multiplier 1.5-2.5 (peaks in Q2 2020, Q1 2021)
     - Suez Canal blockage (Ever Given): March 23-29, 2021
       Effect: Mediterranean + European ports, spike multiplier 3.0 for 3 weeks
     - US West Coast port congestion: Oct 2021 - Mar 2022
       Effect: LA/Long Beach, multiplier 3.5-4.0
     - Red Sea / Houthi crisis: Dec 2023 - present
       Effect: Suez-routing ports (Port Said, Jebel Ali, Singapore),
       multiplier 2.0-2.8, rerouting adds ~14 days Cape of Good Hope bypass
     - Panama Canal drought restrictions: Q3-Q4 2023
       Effect: Panama-adjacent ports (Colon, Manzanillo), multiplier 1.8

  Source citations for each shock: published IMF/UNCTAD/Maersk reports
  (all public, all free). Document these in README.

--- WEATHER MODULATION (calibrated to Open-Meteo) ---
Pull real historical weather from Open-Meteo for each port's coordinates.
Apply delay modulation based on significant wave height (SWH):

  SWH < 1.5m:  weather_multiplier = 1.0 (no effect)
  SWH 1.5-2.5m: weather_multiplier = 1.1
  SWH 2.5-3.5m: weather_multiplier = 1.3
  SWH > 3.5m:  weather_multiplier = 1.6

  Wind speed > 40 knots: additional multiplier 1.2
  Source: IMO operational guidelines + published port weather studies

--- VESSEL CALL VOLUME PER PORT ---
Use real TEU throughput rankings to set call frequency:
  Major hub (>30M TEU/year): ~8,000 vessel calls/year
  Secondary hub (10-30M TEU): ~4,000 vessel calls/year
  Regional port (<10M TEU):  ~1,500 vessel calls/year
  Scale to 5 years (2020-2024) = ~50,000 total records across 60 ports

--- SYNTHETIC RECORD SCHEMA ---
Each row represents one vessel call:

  vessel_call_id        string    unique ID (synthetic)
  port_code             string    UN/LOCODE (real codes)
  port_name             string
  port_country          string
  port_lat              float
  port_lon              float
  arrival_date          date      2020-01-01 to 2024-12-31
  year                  int
  month                 int
  quarter               int
  vessel_type           string    container / bulk / tanker (80% container)
  vessel_size_teu       int       drawn from realistic distribution per port
  scheduled_port_hours  float     from CPPI call size methodology
  actual_port_hours     float     scheduled + delay
  delay_days            float     actual - scheduled (can be 0)
  is_delayed            int       1 if delay_days > 0.5, else 0
  delay_cause           string    weather / congestion / geopolitical / operational
  cppi_score            float     port's CPPI score for that year (from real data)
  gpr_index             float     GPR at port country for that month (real data)
  gpr_category          string    low / medium / high / extreme
  wave_height_m         float     from Open-Meteo (real data)
  wind_speed_knots      float     from Open-Meteo (real data)
  sea_surface_temp_c    float     from Open-Meteo (real data)
  shock_event           string    none / covid / suez / us_congestion / red_sea / panama
  shock_multiplier      float     multiplier applied (for transparency)
  base_delay_mean       float     port's base delay mean (for transparency)
  lpi_score             float     country LPI score (from real data)
  lsci_score            float     country LSCI score (from real data)
  region                string    asia_pacific / europe / middle_east / americas

--- VALIDATION CHECKS ON SYNTHETIC DATA ---
Before using for ML, verify:
  1. Mean delay per port matches published CPPI-implied values (±20%)
  2. Delay spike during COVID period (2021) matches published ~65% schedule reliability
  3. LA/LB ports show correct congestion pattern Oct 2021 - Mar 2022
  4. Red Sea ports show correct pattern Dec 2023+
  5. Overall delay rate ~35-40% (consistent with published industry figures)
  6. Log-normal shape confirmed with Q-Q plot per port

SECTION 5 — ML ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

--- MODEL 1: Binary Delay Classifier (Target A) ---
Task: Predict is_delayed (0/1) for each vessel call
Algorithm: XGBoost classifier
  Why XGBoost: Tabular data, handles mixed types, fast, SHAP-native support
  Why not neural net: Dataset size ~50k rows, tabular structure, interpretability needed
  Why not Random Forest: XGBoost consistently outperforms RF at this scale

Features (inputs):
  - cppi_score (port efficiency — real data)
  - gpr_index (geopolitical risk — real data)
  - wave_height_m (weather — real data)
  - wind_speed_knots (weather — real data)
  - month, quarter (seasonality)
  - vessel_size_teu (vessel characteristics)
  - vessel_type (categorical encoded)
  - lpi_score (logistics quality — real data)
  - lsci_score (connectivity — real data)
  - region (categorical encoded)
  - rolling_delay_rate_30d (port's delay rate last 30 days — derived feature)
  - rolling_delay_rate_90d (port's delay rate last 90 days — derived feature)
  - shock_event (categorical encoded)

Target: is_delayed (binary)
Expected performance: AUC > 0.82, F1 > 0.75
Evaluation: Stratified 80/20 split, cross-validation on time axis
             (train on 2020-2023, test on 2024 — temporal split, not random)
             IMPORTANT: always use temporal split for time series data

--- MODEL 2: Delay Duration Regressor (Target B) ---
Task: Predict delay_days (continuous) for delayed vessel calls
Algorithm: LightGBM regressor
  Why LightGBM: Faster than XGBoost on larger datasets, better on sparse features,
  handles 0-inflated target distribution well

Features: Same as Model 1 plus:
  - predicted_delay_prob from Model 1 (stacking feature)
  - port_avg_delay_12m (trailing average — derived)

Target: delay_days (continuous, log-transformed for training)
Expected performance: RMSE < 1.2 days, MAPE < 18%
Evaluation: Temporal split same as Model 1

--- MODEL 3: Port Risk Score (Target C) ---
Task: Assign a 0-100 risk score to each port for the next 30 days
This is NOT a separate ML model — it's a derived score:

  risk_score = weighted combination of:
    - predicted delay probability for next 30 days (from Model 1): weight 40%
    - predicted mean delay days for next 30 days (from Model 2): weight 30%
    - current GPR index (real-time): weight 15%
    - current wave height anomaly vs seasonal average: weight 10%
    - recent shock event active flag: weight 5%

  Normalise to 0-100 using min-max scaling across all ports
  Colour coding: 0-33 = green, 34-66 = amber, 67-100 = red

--- EXPLAINABILITY LAYER ---
SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) on both XGBoost and LightGBM models:
  - Global feature importance plots
  - Per-prediction waterfall charts (shown in dashboard drilldown)
  - Force plots for top 10 highest-risk vessel calls

This is critical for interviews. "I can explain why the model flagged this port"
is what separates this from a black-box project.
